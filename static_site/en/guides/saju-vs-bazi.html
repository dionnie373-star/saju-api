Saju and Bazi: similarities and differences

Saju and Bazi sound different but build on the same foundations: four pillars, eight characters, five elements. Here is what is the same and where language and practice differ.

Same roots

Bazi (八字, “eight characters”) is the Chinese name, Saju (四柱, “four pillars”) the Korean one. Both terms describe the same basic principle: from the year, month, day and hour of birth you get four pillars with two characters each. The Korean Palja (팔자) also means “eight characters”.

What is the same

  • Ten Heavenly Stems and twelve Earthly Branches
  • Five elements and Yin/Yang
  • The 60-cycle of stem-branch pairs
  • The role of the day stem as a reference point

Where there are differences

  • Language and terms: The same characters are pronounced differently and explained with different technical words (Ohaeng instead of Wuxing, Ilgan instead of Rì Zhǔ).
  • Cultural practice: In Korea Saju reading is a common part of the turn of the year and of life planning, and tends to be conversation-oriented.
  • Time correction: How the hour of birth is determined (time zone, local time, midnight boundary) is not handled the same way in every school.
  • Schools and weighting: In both countries there are different approaches that emphasize elements and cycles differently.

Which version is “right”?

Neither. Both are traditions of interpretation with their own history, not natural science. More important than the name is whether a provider explains transparently how it calculates and states clearly that this is about reflection, not prediction.
