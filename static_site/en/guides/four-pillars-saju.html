The four pillars in Saju explained

Saju literally means “four pillars”. Each pillar has two characters, together making eight characters: Palja. This article explains the structure step by step.

Four pillars, eight characters

The Korean Saju (사주, 四柱) means “four pillars”. These are the year, month, day and hour of birth. Each pillar consists of a Heavenly Stem (above) and an Earthly Branch (below). Four pillars times two characters make eight characters, in Korean Palja (팔자).

Heavenly Stems and Earthly Branches

  • Ten Heavenly Stems: 甲 乙 丙 丁 戊 己 庚 辛 壬 癸. Two each belong to one of the five elements, once Yang, once Yin.
  • Twelve Earthly Branches: 子 丑 寅 卯 辰 巳 午 未 申 酉 戌 亥. These are the twelve animals from Rat to Pig.

Stem and branch are combined into a pair that runs in a cycle of 60 combinations. 2027, for example, is 丁未.

What the four pillars traditionally describe

PillarTraditional association
YearOrigin, family, outer environment
MonthParental home, career, surroundings in adult life; regarded as especially formative
DayThe self; the upper character is the “day master”
HourInner life, later years, outward impression

This is a classical assignment as a thinking frame. It is not a statement about how a life actually unfolds.

Why birth time matters

Without a birth time the hour pillar cannot be determined; you then work with three pillars. In addition, the Saju month does not begin on the first of the calendar month but at solar points (the “solar terms”). That is why the birth chart can differ from the date in the everyday calendar, especially around the change of month.
