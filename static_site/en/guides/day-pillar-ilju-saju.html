The day pillar in Saju (Ilju): what your birthday says

Of the four pillars in a Saju birth chart, the day pillar is the most personal: it depends on your birthday alone. Here you can read what Ilju means, what the day stem traditionally stands for and how to use it for your profile.

What is the day pillar?

The Saju birth chart consists of four pillars: year, month, day and hour. Each pillar has two characters, a Heavenly Stem on top and an Earthly Branch below. The day pillar is called Ilju (일주, 日柱) in Korean. It is calculated from the calendar day, not from the birth year, and repeats in a fixed 60-day rhythm.

Why does the day stem stand for the “self”?

The upper character of the day pillar, the day stem (일간, 日干), is regarded in the classical teaching as the reference point of the whole birth chart. All other characters are read in relation to it: what nourishes it, what challenges it, what resembles it? That is why Saju often says, in shorthand, that the day stem is “you yourself”. This is a traditional image, not a measurement.

And the day branch?

Below the stem stands the day branch, one of the twelve animals. It is traditionally read as the surroundings in which the “self” lives, and is often also linked with partnership and closeness. Which animals exist is explained in Korean zodiac sign (Ddi).

Why can't you quickly work out your Ilju yourself?

The days run in a continuous cycle of 60 combinations that does not follow months or years. You need a calendar table or a calculation from a known starting day. In addition, a Saju day does not begin at the same time of day in every method, which can matter for births around midnight. If you want to be sure, have your profile calculated and check the time of birth.

How do you read the day pillar?

On its own the day stem says little. Only together with the other pillars does a picture emerge, for example which of the five elements are strongly or weakly represented. More about the structure is in The four pillars in Saju.
