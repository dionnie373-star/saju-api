The 60-year cycle in Saju: Gapja and the Korean 60th birthday

Ten Heavenly Stems and twelve animals make a 60-year cycle in the East Asian calendar. Here you learn how it is built, why 2027 is the Fire Sheep and what the Korean Hwangap is about.

How does the 60-year cycle arise?

The ten Heavenly Stems and the twelve Earthly Branches (the animals) are combined in order: stem 1 with branch 1, stem 2 with branch 2 and so on. After ten steps the stems start again, after twelve the branches. Both series meet at the beginning again only after 60 steps. That is the least common multiple of 10 and 12.

This is why there are only 60 combinations and not 120: only pairs occur in which stem and branch have the same polarity (Yang with Yang, Yin with Yin).

Gapja: the beginning

The first combination is called 甲子 (Gapja, 갑자): Yang Wood stem and Rat. In Korean “Gapja” is also used proverbially for a long chapter or span of time. The cycle most recently began in 1984, and the next Gapja year is 2044.

Where does 2027 stand?

2027 is the year 丁未 (Jeongmi), the 44th year of the cycle, the Fire Sheep. The previous year, 2026, is 丙午, the Fire Horse. More in Chinese zodiac 2027. As a reminder: the Saju year begins with Lichun, around 4 February, not on 1 January.

Hwangap: the 60th birthday in Korea

Anyone who turns 60 has gone through the cycle once and is back at the sign of their birth year. In Korea this is traditionally celebrated as Hwangap (환갑, 回甲) with a big family party. In the past, when life expectancy was lower, it counted as a special milestone. Today the 70th birthday is often celebrated instead, but the word has stayed.
