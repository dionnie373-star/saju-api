The Five Elements in Saju, explained simply

Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal and Water are the basic vocabulary of Saju. Here you can read what each element traditionally stands for, how they connect and why it is about patterns, not “good” or “bad” elements.

Five elements, five images

In Korean they are called Ohaeng (오행), in Chinese Wuxing (五行). “Element” is slightly misleading here: they are better understood as five phases or kinds of movement used to describe things.

The generating cycle

In the “generating” cycle each element nourishes the next: Wood feeds Fire, Fire becomes Earth (ash), Earth bears Metal, Metal “collects” Water (the image of dew on cold metal) and Water lets Wood grow.

The controlling cycle

In the “controlling” cycle one element limits another: Wood penetrates Earth, Earth dams Water, Water puts out Fire, Fire melts Metal, Metal cuts Wood. This is meant as an image of balance, not of attack.

Where the elements sit in your birth chart

Each of the four pillars (year, month, day, hour) has two characters, and each character belongs to an element. This gives a distribution across up to eight characters. In interpretation you look at which elements are strongly represented, which are missing and how they relate to each other. The element of the day character (the “day master”) is considered a particularly important reference point.

A common misunderstanding

A “missing” element is not a deficiency in the sense of a verdict. It is a description you can use to think about emphasis. More on this in The four pillars in Saju.
